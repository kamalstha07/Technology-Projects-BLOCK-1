# Part 1 – Hardware vs Software

## Explain Hardware
Hardware refers to the physical components of a computer or electronic system — the tangible parts that perform processing, storage, input, and output.

Write **at least 50 words** explaining what hardware is.
Software is essentially the “mind” of a computer, the part that gives it purpose and personality. When you tap an app on your phone, stream music, write a message, or play a game, you’re interacting with software that someone imagined, designed, and built to make your life a little easier or more enjoyable. It’s not something you can hold in your hands, but you feel its presence in the way it guides your device, responds to your choices, and helps you get things done. In many ways, software is a quiet partner in daily routines, turning raw hardware into something useful, friendly, and meaningful.


## Explain Software
Software is the set of programs, instructions, and data that tell a computer what to do.

Write **at least 50 words** explaining what software is.
Think of software as the rules and directions that tell a computer how to behave. When you tap an app, type a document, or play music, software is what makes those actions possible. It’s created by developers who translate ideas into code, turning machines into helpful tools. In many ways, software is the bridge between human intention and digital action, making technology feel like it understands what we want.

## How Do Hardware and Software Interact?
Write **at least 50 words** describing how they interact.
Hardware and software rely on each other to get anything done. Think of software as the brain giving instructions and hardware as the muscle carrying them out. For example, when you press a key on your keyboard, that physical action sends a signal to software like a text editor, which instantly processes the input and displays the character on your screen. The operating system acts as the bridge between both sides, making sure instructions from your apps are cleanly passed down to the processor and memory.
